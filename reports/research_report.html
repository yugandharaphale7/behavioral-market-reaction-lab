Abstract

This project investigates observational associations between financial-news sentiment, novelty, repetition, information intensity, and subsequent market behavior. It does not claim causal effects or a trading edge.

Research question

What types of financial news appear to produce the strongest and most persistent market reactions? Is the evidence consistent with overreaction, underreaction, attention effects, novelty effects, or sentiment persistence?

Dataset

Primary source: Kaggle Daily Financial News for 6000+ Stocks. The source description reports approximately 843,062 processed analyst-rating records and 6,193 tickers; exact post-filter counts are populated only after the user supplies the downloaded file.

Methodology

The pipeline normalizes timestamps and tickers, removes duplicates, computes an interpretable lexicon sentiment score, calculates historical-only TF-IDF novelty and repetition, aligns daily news with OHLCV prices, and estimates forward returns, abnormal returns versus SPY, rolling volatility, event CARs, correlations, bootstrap intervals, fixed-effects regressions, and robustness checks.

Limitations

Timing precision, sentiment lexicon coverage, price/news alignment, overlapping events, survivorship, missing delisted securities, and observational confounding limit interpretation. Statistical significance is not causal evidence.
